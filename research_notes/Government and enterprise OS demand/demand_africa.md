## Which African markets have real, funded demand for a low-cost Linux desktop with cloud services, and who are the buyers?

### Takeaway
No verified evidence was gathered this session of any funded African government or education programme that mandates or procures a Linux desktop. Likely buyers (ministries of education, ICT agencies, donors) are named only as unverified leads below.

### Cited Findings
- OLPC's "Give 1 Get 1" (2008) reportedly made XO laptops available to Ethiopia, Ghana, Nigeria, Madagascar and Rwanda; no African deployment numbers, OS versions or outcomes are given in the article. Source quality: secondary (Wikipedia) — [Wikipedia: One Laptop per Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Laptop_per_Child)
- Rwanda OLPC: a September 2009 photo caption shows XO laptops at Kagugu Primary School, Kigali, running Scratch. Seats not stated. Secondary — [Wikipedia: One Laptop per Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Laptop_per_Child)
- Nigeria OLPC: in 2007 XO laptops were found to contain inappropriate material and OLPC Nigeria announced filtering. Secondary — [Wikipedia: One Laptop per Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Laptop_per_Child)
- OLPC shipped about 3 million laptops globally by 2015, about 35% to Uruguay; i.e. Africa was a small share. Secondary — [Wikipedia: One Laptop per Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Laptop_per_Child)
- Mara Group (Ashish Thakkar, Dubai-based, founded 1996) operates in 24 countries, 22 in Africa, about 11,000 staff as of 2014. The article says nothing about Mara's Rwanda laptop/phone assembly or any OS. Secondary — [Wikipedia: Mara Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mara_Group)
- The Wikipedia "Linux adoption" article names no African government, school or enterprise deployment. Absence of evidence in a general article only, not proof of none — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)

### Inferences
- The absence of any African entry in the standard Linux-adoption overview suggests there is no France/Munich-style flagship African government Linux migration that is widely documented. Weak inference, based on one article.
- Education device programmes (not ministries' office desktops) are the most plausible demand channel, but this is not verified here.

## What entry paths and requirements apply (local partners, assemblers, donors' eligibility rules)?

### Takeaway
Nothing verified this session. Only general pattern from the one verified item: assemblers/conglomerates like Mara operate across many African countries, so a partner route via pan-African assemblers is conceivable, but no OS or procurement requirement is confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Mara Group is a multinational conglomerate active in 22 African locations (as of 2014 data), indicating pan-African distribution reach — [Wikipedia: Mara Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mara_Group)

### Inferences
- Typical African public procurement involves national e-procurement portals, local-content or local-registration preferences, and donor rules (World Bank, AfDB eligibility). These are general knowledge, NOT verified for any specific country here.
