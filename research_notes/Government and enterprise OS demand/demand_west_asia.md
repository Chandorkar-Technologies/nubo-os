## How large and real is demand in the Gulf and wider region for a locally hosted, Arabic-capable Linux desktop with cloud services, and what are the entry paths?

### Takeaway
Verified evidence of Linux desktop mandates is only for Turkey (Pardus/ETAP, state-backed). For the Gulf I found no verified Linux desktop programme in this pass; Gulf demand appears to be for sovereign cloud/AI and compliance-driven hosting rather than desktop OS replacement (UNVERIFIED). Treat Gulf desktop-Linux demand as unproven.

### Cited Findings
- Turkey: Pardus is developed by TUBITAK (initiated 2003 via UEKAE) "with support from the government of Turkey"; main focus office work in Turkish government agencies; desktop options Xfce, GNOME, Pardus ETAP — [Wikipedia Pardus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardus_(operating_system)) (secondary).
- Pardus project site (primary, project's own marketing) lists institutional success stories: Ministry of Health (open-source transformation project), Diyanet (Directorate of Religious Affairs), AFAD (disaster agency), Ziraat Participation Bank, ISKI (Istanbul water authority); current release Pardus 25.2 (GNOME and Xfce); "3.0+ million total downloads"; ETAP = Interactive Board interface project for classroom whiteboards, with an installed-board statistics page (number not retrieved) — [pardus.org.tr](https://www.pardus.org.tr/en/). Seat counts not stated on the fetched page.
- Wikipedia Linux adoption page contains nothing on Middle East/Caucasus — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption) (confirms absence of well-known coverage).

### Inferences
- Only Turkey has a state-sponsored, domestically developed distro with named public-sector users; it is also a closed field for foreign vendors (a domestic distribution is the policy), so Nubo is unlikely to win Turkish government seats; Turkey is a competitor reference, not a market.
- Gulf data-sovereignty rules (if as recalled) bind cloud hosting more than the desktop OS: Nubo's cloud mail/files would be the part needing in-country hosting; the OS itself is free.
