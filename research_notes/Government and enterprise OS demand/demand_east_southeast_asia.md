## 1. How large and real is government and enterprise demand in the region, and which markets are open vs closed to a foreign-run Linux distribution?

### Takeaway
China is a large, state-mandated domestic-OS market (Kylin, UOS) that is closed to foreign-run distributions. Outside China, the only verified signals in this session are a 2019 South Korean announcement and the long-running state-controlled Red Star OS in North Korea. Beyond those, I verified nothing concrete.

### Cited Findings
- By 2019, Kylin and NeoKylin together were reported to hold about 90% of the Chinese government sector; Kylin ships pre-installed on most government-purchased computers. Secondary source (Wikipedia), and the 90% figure is dated 2019 — [Wikipedia: Kylin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylin_(operating_system)); [Wikipedia: openKylin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenKylin)
- Kylin began in 2001 at the National University of Defense Technology under the 863 Program, with the stated aim of reducing foreign technology dependence. NeoKylin was formed in December 2010 with China Standard Software, targeting government, defence and energy. Kylin V10 launched August 2020, and the open-source variant openKylin was released July 2022. Secondary — [Wikipedia: openKylin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenKylin)
- Dell reported in 2015 that 42% of PCs sold in China ran NeoKylin (likely as a pre-installed free-OS channel, not active use; treat with caution). Secondary — [Wikipedia: Kylin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylin_(operating_system))
- Siping (a northeastern Chinese city) moved computers from Windows to NeoKylin, reported by Bloomberg in 2014 (the Wikipedia article cites Bloomberg; the original was not fetched). Secondary — [Wikipedia: Kylin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylin_(operating_system))
- Deepin/UOS: Deepin Technology became a wholly owned subsidiary of UnionTech (统信软件) on 1 January 2020. Deepin reported more than 3 million users and 80 million downloads (Dec 2022, company figure, mostly consumer or global). Loongson, UnionTech and Sunway partnered in 2020 to promote Deepin to reduce dependence on Microsoft Windows. Secondary — [Wikipedia: Deepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepin)
- A Wikipedia entry reads: in August 2026 China's Ministry of State Security ordered some government entities to switch from "Windows 10 China Government edition" to domestic Linux distributions. No seat numbers. The underlying citations (refs 148-149) could not be viewed, so this is NOT independently verified. Secondary, unverified, and the most important item to re-check — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)
- South Korea: in May 2019 the government announced it was looking to migrate major government systems to Linux ahead of Windows 7 end of support. No seat numbers in the source. Secondary — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)
- North Korea: Red Star OS is the state-approved desktop and server OS (v3.0 in April 2012; a v4.0 was reported 2019-2020), with built-in surveillance and watermarking features. Closed market and sanctions-restricted; not a commercial opportunity. Secondary — [Wikipedia: Red Star OS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_OS)
- Indonesia: BlankOn is a Debian-based Indonesian distribution supported by YPLI since 2004. Latest stable was 11.0 "Uluwatu" (May 2018), with a 12 beta in April 2021, so it is effectively low-activity. The IGOS government link was not confirmed in this fetch. Secondary — [Wikipedia: BlankOn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlankOn_Linux)
- The Wikipedia Linux-adoption page showed no entries for Japan, Taiwan, Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand or the Philippines. This reflects that page's coverage only and is not evidence that no programmes exist — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)

### Inferences
- UNVERIFIED, from background knowledge, to be checked: China's "Document 79" (2020) and reported 2022 central-government instructions aimed at replacing foreign PC software in central SOEs by about 2027 (widely reported by Bloomberg/FT in Dec 2022), alongside the Xinchuang (信创) procurement lists, MLPS (classified protection) and commercial-cryptography requirements. If these hold, a foreign-run, Ubuntu-based distribution cannot realistically enter China government or SOE procurement; consumer and individual use remains open but is of low value.
- Korea's Gooroom (a government-sponsored Debian-based OS from KISA/MSIT era) and the HamoniKR community distribution, plus the CC/CSAP certification regime for public-sector products, are known from background knowledge but were NOT verified in this session; the Korea route would require local certification and a local entity.
- If the 2026 China MSS report is accurate, it indicates demand is policy-driven and not open to outsiders, so it informs the "closed markets" list but not Nubo's addressable market.

## 2. What entry paths and requirements apply (local partners, certifications, hosting)?

### Takeaway
No requirement could be verified from sources in this session. Background expectations are listed below as UNVERIFIED and need confirmation.

### Cited Findings
- None verified. The only link to procurement mechanics is the unverified Aug 2026 China MSS instruction for government entities to move to domestic Linux distributions — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)

### Inferences
- UNVERIFIED expectations to confirm: China requires a domestic vendor on Xinchuang lists, MLPS and commercial-cryptography approvals and in-country data residency, which effectively excludes foreign-run distros. Korea requires CC/CSAP certification and typically a local entity. Singapore GovTech and Japan government cloud procurement run through approved-vendor frameworks. Southeast Asian markets (Indonesia TKDN local-content rules, Vietnam domestic-software preference and data localisation laws) probably favour local partners. None of this was confirmed here.
- Paid cloud accounts (mail.nubo.email) for organisations would face data-residency issues in China, Vietnam and Indonesia; consumer-first positioning avoids the procurement barrier.

### Gaps
- No tender, certification or local-entity requirement was verified for any country.
