## Which American markets have real, funded demand for a Linux desktop with cloud services, and who are the buyers?

### Takeaway
In the sources reachable, Americas Linux-desktop demand is mostly legacy and weak: Venezuela's Canaima is the only state-mandated, large-scale deployment (still maintained); Cuba's Nova never displaced Windows; Uruguay's Plan Ceibal moved off Linux to Windows 10. The 2025-26 sovereignty wave found in the sources is European (France, Denmark, Germany, Netherlands). No Americas equivalent (a funded government-wide Linux migration) was verified in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Venezuela: Canaima GNU/Linux (Debian-based) first released 18 Oct 2007; made default OS of the public administration on 14 Mar 2011 under presidential decree 3,390 (priority to free/open-source tech in public administration); "Canaima Educativo" delivered about 6 million laptops to schoolchildren by 2017; CANTV, CNTI, Edelca involved; version 8.0 released 18 Oct 2024; status: maintained, active. Buyer: state (education ministry, state telco). Quality: secondary. — [Wikipedia: Canaima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaima_(operating_system))
- Cuba: Nova Linux launched Feb 2009 at UCI Havana; goal Nova on 90% of workplaces; early 2011 UCI announced migration of 8,000+ computers; v9.0 released 2 Oct 2023; website appears abandoned as of Feb 2026 (expired TLS certificate since Aug 2024); Windows remains dominant on almost every Cuban computer. Status: stalled/limited. Quality: secondary. — [Wikipedia: Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_(operating_system))
- Uruguay: Plan Ceibal started 2007 (pilot Villa Cardal); about 450,000 laptops to primary students/teachers in four years; 1 million devices by 2013; original XO laptops ran Sugar (Linux-based), later Android and Windows 10; most recent conventional laptops use Windows 10 as primary OS with Linux compatibility. Status: reversed from Linux in practice. Quality: secondary. — [Wikipedia: Plan Ceibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_Ceibal)
- Argentina (Misiones province): January 2021 announcement of GobMis GNU/Linux, a Devuan-based distro for government offices; seat count not stated; current status not verified. — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)
- Brazil: federal government under Lula and "Software Livre Brasil" advocated Linux in schools, public departments and desktops (historic, no seat numbers in source). — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)
- Canada (Quebec): 2010 Superior Court ruling that the province broke procurement law by spending about CAD 720,000 migrating 800 workstations to Windows Vista without evaluating alternatives (required for spend over CAD 25,000). Historic precedent that procurement law can force evaluation of open-source alternatives; not a recent sovereignty policy. — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)
- US education/enterprise: Chromebooks became the most popular computer in US K-12 by 2016; Chrome Enterprise launched 2017; ChromeOS Flex (installable on old PCs) launched July 2022; Google confirmed July 2025 that ChromeOS will merge into Android-based platform (codename Aluminium OS), replacing Linux-based ChromeOS. Implication: the largest "Linux-kernel" desktop buyer base (K-12) is tied to Google's roadmap. — [Wikipedia: ChromeOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrome_OS)
- Positivo Tecnologia (Brazil): sells computers to public-sector via public procurement since 1990; 2025 revenue R$3.354 billion; the page does not mention Linux preloads. — [Wikipedia: Positivo Tecnologia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivo_Tecnologia)
- Ubuntu: Dell, Lenovo and HP have offered Ubuntu laptops/desktops (general statement, no region/programme detail). — [Wikipedia: Ubuntu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubuntu_(operating_system))
- Context, outside the Americas: France DINUM (April 2026) mandated ministries to plan removal of extra-European dependencies by autumn 2026, migrating 2.5 million workstations Windows to Linux; Denmark (June 2025) phasing out Office 365/Windows for LibreOffice/Linux. Demonstrates the sovereignty narrative Nubo can borrow, but no Americas analogue verified. — [Wikipedia: Digital sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_sovereignty)

### Inferences
- Realistic funded buyers in the Americas are likely education ministries (laptop programmes), state telcos/utilities, and defence/security bodies, but only Venezuela and (historically) Uruguay are evidenced here. Treat the Americas as a thin, mostly consumer/prosumer and developer market for Nubo near-term, with sovereignty-driven public demand unproven.
- Free-OS-only vendors have a poor track record in Latin American state programmes (Cuba Nova, Ceibal), suggesting government deals hinge on hardware bundles and Windows/Android compatibility rather than the OS.

## What entry paths and requirements apply (integrators, local partners, certifications)?

### Takeaway
Not verifiable from what was reachable. The only evidenced procurement-law fact is Quebec's 2010 ruling that large public IT spends require evaluation of alternatives, and Brazil's Positivo-style OEMs sell to the public sector through public procurement; the specific portals and certifications could not be confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Quebec courts enforced a rule that spend over CAD 25,000 requires search for alternatives, supporting a "request an open-source evaluation" entry argument. — [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption)
- Positivo has supplied public-sector bodies via public procurement since 1990, so Brazilian local OEMs are existing channel partners for education/government hardware. — [Wikipedia: Positivo Tecnologia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivo_Tecnologia)

### Inferences
- Likely requirements (to verify): FedRAMP/FIPS 140 for US federal cloud services (relevant to Nubo's mail/files cloud, which is Indian-hosted, so a data-residency hurdle); Spanish/Portuguese localisation; local integrator/OEM partner for tenders. These are inferences, not sourced.

### Gaps
- Portal specifics (SAM.gov, GSA, CanadaBuys, COMPRAS.gov.br, national portals), mandatory vendor requirements, integrator lists: not verified.
