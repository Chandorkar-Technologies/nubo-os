# South Asia and Central Asia: market opportunity and OS-distribution requirements (as of October 2026)

Method note: search budget for the session ran out partway, so legal and payment coverage is thinner than demographics and OS share. Items marked "(background, not verified this session)" come from my prior knowledge and have no fetched source; the report writer should treat them as leads, not facts. Population and internet figures are DataReportal Digital 2026 (data as of October 2025, published November 2025). StatCounter figures are the September 2026 desktop-OS pages. StatCounter country data is sample-based and noisy for small countries; "Linux" in particular is inflated or unstable in several markets (see flags).

## Which countries are the best targets and why (India as home market, plus overall ranking)

### Takeaway
India is the clear primary market: about 1.03 billion internet users, a PC market growing 12% YoY, rising new-PC prices pushing buyers to refurbished machines, an unusually high StatCounter Linux share (about 10%, likely partly noisy), and a domestic payments/legal regime that a locally incorporated company can serve. Rank after India: Bangladesh and Nepal (Medium, large/young populations, Bengali/Nepali script needs, FX friction), Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan (Medium, high connectivity, now workable on data law, but Russian/local-language and payment-rail work), Pakistan (Medium with high risk), Sri Lanka and the small markets (Low), Turkmenistan and Afghanistan (Blocked or near-blocked).

### Inferences
- Rising memory-driven prices for new PCs plus Win10 end-of-support create a pool of older machines that cannot run Windows 11 and are good Ubuntu/GNOME candidates; this is an inference, the share of unsupported PCs in India was not found.
- Ranking: India (High); Bangladesh, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Pakistan, Nepal (Medium or Medium/Low); Sri Lanka, Bhutan, Maldives, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan (Low); Afghanistan, Turkmenistan (Blocked). Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan have the highest connectivity (93.4% and 89.0%) but small total populations; Uzbekistan (33m users) is the larger.

## What local requirements change the product (languages, offline use, low-end hardware, data residency)

### Takeaway
Language and input support is the largest product lever: Indic scripts for India/Nepal, Bengali, Sinhala/Tamil, Urdu Nastaliq and Perso-Arabic RTL, and Cyrillic plus Kazakh/Uzbek-specific letters for Central Asia. Data residency matters mainly for India's Significant Data Fiduciaries (not applicable to a small start-up unless designated), Kazakhstan (physical in-country storage), Bangladesh (restricted/confidential data), and Sri Lanka (cross-border instruments); Uzbekistan has eased.

### Cited Findings
- India DPDP Rules were notified 14 Nov 2025 with an 18-month phased timeline; the Centre can specify data that Significant Data Fiduciaries may process only in India — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014&reg=3&lang=2); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1830402/dpdp-act-and-rules-2025-the-2026-compliance-milestones-businesses-cant-afford-to-miss)
- Kazakhstan: personal data of citizens must be stored on servers in Kazakhstan; extraterritorial reach unclear for foreign firms without presence — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=df9f87c9-a87d-4246-a3f5-086421bf6117); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/-/media/files/publication/outside-publication/article/2024/data-localization-laws-overview-kazakhstan.pdf)
- Uzbekistan Senate approved easing on 6 Feb 2026: only biometric, genetic and telecoms data must stay local — [Times of Central Asia](https://timesca.com/uzbekistan-eases-data-localization-rules-to-support-global-payment-platforms/)
- Bangladesh PDPO 2025: restricted/confidential data stored in Bangladesh; adequacy-based transfers; reported Jan 2026 amendment removed broad localisation for tech firms; compliance deadline about May 2027 — [DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/opinion/bangladesh-data-protection-ordinance-2025-key-part-two); [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/ikh9acxpg5)
- Sri Lanka PDPA: Authority operational since 17 Jul 2023; cross-border transfers need instruments such as BCRs and agreements; substantive duties awaiting commencement per 2026 guides — [DLA Piper](https://dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=LK&t=transfer); [BiometricUpdate](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202511/sri-lanka-nods-personal-data-protection-act-amendments)
- Pakistan has no data-protection law in force; PDPB 2023 pending; transfers to India require justification — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=PK)
- Nepal: Privacy Act 2075 and Regulation 2077 cover personal data of residents — [LawAlpine](https://lawalpine.com/blog/privacy-laws-in-nepal)
- BOSS supports 19 Indian languages, a benchmark for Nubo's language coverage in India — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Operating_System_Solutions)

### Inferences
- Product must ship with full CLDR/Noto coverage for Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Gujarati, Gurmukhi, Odia, Sinhala, Thaana, Tibetan (Dzongkha), Arabic-script (Urdu Nastaliq, Pashto, Dari), and Cyrillic, with phonetic and legacy input layouts; this follows from the language list, not from a cited source (language lists are background knowledge).
- For India, run mail/files on Indian-region servers by default; that handles DPDP, CERT-In and user trust at low cost.
- Kazakhstan would require an in-country hosting partner before offering paid cloud accounts; Bangladesh and Sri Lanka require legal review before onboarding residents; consider launching OS download only (no cloud account) in those countries first.
- Low-end hardware and offline use: no country-specific evidence found; the refurb-PC thesis implies tuning for 4 GB RAM and spinning disks (inference).

- Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Bhutan, Maldives data-protection and localisation rules: not researched.
- Whether Uzbekistan's February 2026 amendment has entered into force.
- Pakistan VPN registration and encryption rules; Sri Lanka commencement date: not found.

## Where distribution or payment is blocked or high risk

### Takeaway
Afghanistan and Turkmenistan should be treated as blocked: Afghanistan had a nationwide internet shutdown in Sept/Oct 2025 that froze banking and remittances, and Turkmenistan runs a pervasive censorship and surveillance regime. Pakistan is high risk because of legal flux and India-origin company sensitivity; Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka carry foreign-exchange and compliance uncertainty that was not verified.

### Cited Findings
- Taliban imposed a nationwide internet blackout in late Sept 2025 (after a fibre ban in five northern provinces), later denying it; banking, ATMs and remittances failed — [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2025/10/afghanistans-digital-blackout-a-self-inflicted-economic-crisis/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/03/asia/afghanistan-internet-shutdown-intl-hnk-dst); [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/afghanistan/afghanistan-blackout-taliban-denies-nationwide-internet-ban-women-rcna234865)
- Afghanistan has 11.3m internet users (25.5%) — [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-afghanistan)
- Turkmenistan: state-controlled "Turkmenet" censored intranet; at least 122,000 domains blocked (2023); significant surveillance — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_Turkmenistan)
- Pakistan PECA Amendment 2025 created SMPRA with powers to require social platforms to register or face blocking/penalties; stated not to apply to individual users or ordinary businesses — [Lex Mundi](https://www.lexmundi.com/guides/data-privacy-guide/jurisdictions/asia-pacific/pakistan/)
- Nepal: Foreign Exchange (Regulation and Management) Bill 2026 is being drafted to modernise NRB's tools — [Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/business/why-nepal-is-rewriting-its-foreign-exchange-laws-everything-you-need-to-know/)

### Inferences
- A cloud subscription would be unservable in Afghanistan and effectively in Turkmenistan; the free OS ISO is still downloadable from outside, but no support or billing is realistic. Sanctions exposure for Afghanistan (Taliban-linked designations) was not verified.
- Pakistan: despite the large user base, treat it as phased/pilot with local legal counsel; avoid India-based hosting of Pakistani residents' data given the transfers-to-India scrutiny noted by DLA Piper.
- Using an international processor (Stripe/Razorpay-type) will work for cards in India, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan in principle but this was not verified.

### Gaps
- Sanctions/export-control position by country (US/EU/UN; Ubuntu crypto export rules): not researched.
- Payments for every country except the Uzbekistan PayPal-related opening: not verified.
- Pakistan VPN licensing, Bangladesh BTRC rules, Central Asia platform/telecom licences: not researched.
- Hardware type approval (BIS/WPC in India, PTA/BTRC elsewhere): not researched; only relevant to future bundles.
- India refurbished/PC-for-education programmes and public-sector Linux in Pakistan, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka: not found (search budget exhausted).
