Research constraint note: the session web-search budget ran out after about 7 searches, so all later work was via direct page fetches. Items marked "[background, not verified]" come from general knowledge and carry no source; treat them as leads, not facts. "Not verified" means I could not confirm it.

## 1. Country table (population, internet, desktop OS, languages, law, opportunity)

### Takeaway
Australia and New Zealand are the only Oceania markets with meaningful paying-consumer scale (about 32 million people, 96-97% online, card-based payments, Stripe supported). Both have modern privacy law but no data-localisation mandate found. Every Pacific island state is tiny (largest is PNG at 10.8 million but only 24% online) and most lack comprehensive data-protection law (Vanuatu is the exception). Pacific states are a free-download audience, not a revenue market.

#### Data protection and localisation
- Australia: Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 passed 29 Nov 2024; added a statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy (in force six months after Royal Assent on 10 Dec 2024, i.e. about June 2025), new OAIC enforcement powers, civil penalty tiers and infringement notices, and requires a Children's Online Privacy Code by 10 Dec 2026; exposure draft released March 2026 — [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/pasing-of-bill-a-significant-step-for-australias-privacy-law); [Attorney-General's draft code release](https://ministers.ag.gov.au/media-centre/draft-childrens-online-privacy-code-released-31-03-2026); [MinterEllison summary](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/oaics-childrens-online-privacy-code-what-to-expect). The code covers apps, games, educational tools and websites likely accessed by children. Privacy Act applies to organisations carrying on business in Australia (extraterritorial reach) [background, not verified; the Act's small-business exemption and APP 8 cross-border disclosure accountability are also background]. No general data-localisation rule for mail/files found; localisation exists only for specific sectors (My Health Records, some government data) [background, not verified]. Consumer Data Right applies to designated sectors (banking, energy) and does not cover a mail/file provider [background, not verified].
- New Zealand: Privacy Act 2020 with new IPP3A (notification when collecting personal information from someone other than the individual) in force 1 May 2026 — [Justice NZ](https://www.justice.govt.nz/about/news-and-media/news/new-privacy-information-principle-in/); [PwC NZ](https://www.pwc.co.nz/insights-and-publications/2026-publications/new-indirect-collection-notification-obligations-privacy-act-2025.html). IPP12 cross-border disclosure rule and Privacy Act 2020 extraterritorial application to overseas agencies "carrying on business" in NZ: [background, not verified]. No localisation mandate found.
- Vanuatu: Data Protection and Privacy Act in effect from 2 Jan 2025, covers public and private sectors, extraterritorial application, cross-border transfer rules, data-subject rights — [DataGuidance Vanuatu](https://www.dataguidance.com/jurisdictions/vanuatu) (via search summary). It is reported as the only Pacific island nation with comprehensive data-protection legislation in force; verify at primary source before relying on it.
- Fiji: no comprehensive data-protection law as of the sources found; Telecommunications Promulgation 2008 s54(1)(e) requires providers to keep consumer info confidential; national digital ID system announced 2025 — same search summary (primary sources not fetched). Fiji Cybercrime Act 2021 and Online Safety Act 2018: [background, not verified].
- Samoa: National Digital Identification Act 2024 exists; no standalone data-protection law found. Tonga and PNG: no dedicated law found in sources (not verified either way).
- Solomon Islands, Kiribati, Tuvalu, Nauru, Palau, Marshall Islands, FSM, Cook Islands, Niue: not verified (not researched; assume no comprehensive regime unless confirmed).
- French Polynesia and New Caledonia: French territories; whether GDPR/French Loi Informatique et Libertes applies directly (New Caledonia and French Polynesia are generally outside EU law as OCTs but French law on data protection is extended) is not verified. Guam: US territory, US federal law applies; no federal comprehensive privacy statute; Guam-specific rule not verified.

#### Other legal requirements for shipping an OS plus cloud account
- Australia smart-device standards: Cyber Security (Security Standards for Smart Devices) Rules 2025 commenced 4 March 2026; apply to consumer connectable products acquired in Australia; exclude smartphones, tablets, desktop and laptop computers, road vehicles and therapeutic goods; require unique passwords, vulnerability-disclosure contact, disclosed security-update period, statement of compliance retained five years — [Home Affairs](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/about-us/our-portfolios/cyber-security/security-standards-for-smart-devices) (page returned 403 to my fetch; content is from search-result excerpts of Home Affairs, [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/30582-cyber-security-security-standards-for-smart-devices-rules-2025-enter-into-force), [Nemko](https://www.nemko.com/blog/mandatory-cybersecurity-australias-new-regulations-from-4-march-2026)). Consequence: a software-only Linux desktop OS is outside it; a future Nubo hardware bundle that is a laptop/desktop is also excluded; a Nubo-branded router, NAS, camera or Nextcloud box would be in scope.
- Australia Assistance and Access Act 2018 (TAR/TAN/TCN powers that can be issued to designated communications providers, including those supplying to Australia from overseas, and that cannot require a systemic weakness): Home Affairs page returned 403; entirely [background, not verified]. Practical implication: a mail/file host serving Australians is a potential "designated communications provider".
- Australia online safety: eSafety industry codes and Online Safety Act, including whether OS providers are covered by age-assurance codes: fetch timed out / 403, not verified. Also not verified: Australia's under-16 social media minimum age law (in force Dec 2025) scope; it targets social media platforms, and a mail/file service is likely out of scope [inference].
- Australia and NZ consumer law (Australian Consumer Law guarantees apply to digital services supplied to Australians; NZ Consumer Guarantees Act and Fair Trading Act): [background, not verified].
- Accessibility: Australian Disability Discrimination Act and NZ Accessibility Charter apply mainly to government; no consumer-OS-specific mandate found. EU European Accessibility Act is relevant only for EU, not Oceania. Not verified.
- Tax on digital services: Australia GST on imported digital products and NZ GST on remote services (non-resident suppliers must register above a threshold: A$75,000 and NZ$60,000 respectively, rates 10% and 15%) — [background, not verified]; ATO and IRD pages returned 403/404 on fetch.
- Hardware type approval (only if bundling devices): Australia RCM/ACMA and NZ RSM radio and EMC compliance, Australian Consumer Law product safety; Pacific states typically rely on their own telecom regulator or accept FCC/CE/RCM marks: [background, not verified].
- Sanctions/export: none specific to Oceania countries; no OFAC/EU country-wide program found for any Oceania state (see global baseline). Caution: "New Caledonia, French Polynesia, Guam" follow French/US law respectively [background].

#### Payment and billing feasibility
- Stripe merchant countries include Australia and New Zealand; Fiji, PNG and all other Pacific states are not on the Stripe merchant list; India is listed only as "Preview" — [Stripe global](https://stripe.com/global). Note: this concerns where the merchant is based, not where customers live; "Once Stripe is supported in your country/region, you'll be able to sell to customers anywhere in the world" (same page). A Nubo entity in India therefore cannot rely on a Stripe merchant account in India (preview only at fetch time), but could use another entity.
- Razorpay: Indian businesses can enable international payments (160+ currencies; international debit/credit cards; PayPal; subscriptions supported) after KYC and video verification — [Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments/). Pacific-country-level card acceptance, and whether AUD/NZD/FJD/PGK/XPF settle: not verified. Subscriptions are supported for international payments per the page, but recurring card-mandate rules for foreign cards under RBI: not verified.
- PayPal country coverage for Pacific states: page content not retrievable; not verified.
- Fiji and PNG consumers: card/online-payment penetration is low; a 2021 UNCDF report on digital/e-commerce payments in the Pacific exists ([UNCDF](https://www.uncdf.org/Download/AdminFileWithFilename?id=15514&cultureId=127&filename=2021the-opportunity-for-digital-and-e-commerce-payments-in-the-pacific-regionpdf)) but I did not read it; claims about mobile-money-first economies are [background, not verified].

### Inferences

- The Home Affairs smart-device rule does not touch a PC OS or laptop bundle.

## 2. Narrative notes: Australia, New Zealand, Pacific group

### Takeaway
Australia and New Zealand are worth a localised launch (AUD/NZD pricing, local-law privacy notice, GST handling); the Pacific is best covered with a single "Pacific English" listing, no local entity, and acceptance of foreign-card payments only.

### Cited Findings
- Australia: privacy reform tranche 1 is in force and tranche 2 is pending; the Children's Online Privacy Code is due by 10 Dec 2026 and would bite if Nubo serves under-18 users via family accounts — [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/pasing-of-bill-a-significant-step-for-australias-privacy-law), [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/oaics-childrens-online-privacy-code-what-to-expect).
- Australia: PCs are excluded from the smart-device standards, which commenced 4 March 2026 — [Nemko](https://www.nemko.com/blog/mandatory-cybersecurity-australias-new-regulations-from-4-march-2026).
- New Zealand: IPP3A took effect 1 May 2026; the Privacy Act is mostly principles-based and aligned to GDPR-like notice obligations — [Justice NZ](https://www.justice.govt.nz/about/news-and-media/news/new-privacy-information-principle-in/).
- Pacific: Vanuatu has a new comprehensive law (2025); most other states have none — [DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/jurisdictions/vanuatu).

### Inferences
- Because Nubo's mail hosting is outside Australia/NZ (India or elsewhere) it should publish a privacy policy meeting APP 1/5/8 and IPP12 style disclosures; no hard localisation requirement was found, but this was not exhaustively checked.
- Pacific: low legal risk, low revenue.

### Gaps
- Whether OAIC treats a free-OS-plus-paid-cloud publisher as subject to the Privacy Act given the small-business exemption (A$3m turnover); not verified.

## 3. Global baseline (applies in every country)

### Takeaway
No Oceania country is sanctioned, so global rules reduce to: encryption export classification (open-source exempt, notify if non-standard crypto), sanctions screening, GPL obligations, Canonical trademark removal for a rebranded Ubuntu derivative, and payment-processor coverage.

### Cited Findings
- US EAR encryption: under 15 CFR 742.15, publicly available encryption source code classified 5D002 is not subject to the EAR (even if commercial use carries fees); if it provides "non-standard cryptography" you must notify BIS (crypt@bis.doc.gov) and NSA (enc@nsa.gov) with the internet location; other encryption items need a licence or License Exception ENC (740.17) — [eCFR 15 CFR 742.15](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-15?part=742&section=742.15). Nubo is an Indian company: whether the EAR applies depends on US-origin content (Ubuntu packages are US/UK-origin) [background, not verified]; Canonical publishes its own ENC classification [background, not verified]. Source code or binaries made by Nubo itself are subject to Indian export rules [not verified].
- OFAC comprehensive programs page shows Cuba (updated 29 Sep 2026), Iran (updated 1 Oct 2026) and North Korea (updated 12 Mar 2026) as active programs; no separate Syria program is listed as of the fetch, only the "Promoting Accountability for Assad and Regional Stabilization Sanctions" (PAARSS, updated 24 Sep 2026); Crimea/DNR/LNR fall under the Ukraine-/Russia-related program (updated 8 May 2026) — [OFAC programs page](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information). So the user's brief list ("Syria") is outdated: Syria no longer appears as a comprehensive program per this page; confirm with counsel before enabling Syria signups. Whether OFAC applies to an Indian company serving non-US persons: depends on US nexus (US-dollar clearing, US-based processors, US persons); general licences for personal communications (31 CFR 560.540 Iran; 515.578 Cuba) and software incident to personal communications: [background, not verified].
- EU/UK sanctions on Russia/Belarus restricting software/cloud services: fetch of the Council page timed out; not verified (EU Regulation 833/2014 Art. 5n bars software/IT services to the Russian government and certain entities; broader cloud/IT-service bans came in later packages [background, not verified]).
- GPL: source-offer duties apply when distributing binaries (provide source or a written offer valid for three years, include licence texts); not fetched, [background, not verified]. Practical point: Nubo ISOs distribute GPL binaries so should host the matching source (Ubuntu archive plus Nubo patches).
- Canonical trademark: modified Ubuntu may be redistributed with Ubuntu trademarks only if approved/certified/provided by Canonical (a paid licence may be needed); otherwise remove all Ubuntu trademarks and logos, recompile source for your own binaries, and not market it as Ubuntu; unmodified redistribution is free — [Canonical IP policy](https://canonical.com/legal/intellectual-property-policy).
- Codec/patent licensing (H.264/H.265/AAC/MP3): not fetched; not verified. Ubuntu ships limited codecs, with extras via ubuntu-restricted-extras [background, not verified].
- Payment processors: Stripe merchant support is limited to about 46 listed countries plus Paystack markets; India is "Preview" — [Stripe global](https://stripe.com/global). Razorpay offers international payments from India with 160+ currencies, available regions India, Malaysia, Singapore, US — [Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments/). PayPal list: not retrievable, not verified.

### Inferences
- Nubo should state in its terms that it does not provide cloud accounts in Cuba, Iran, North Korea and the Crimea/DNR/LNR regions (and Syria pending legal review) and should block signups from those via IP/billing country.
- Since the OS image contains no Ubuntu marks (per the repo's rebrand work), the trademark rule is satisfied as long as rebranding is complete, including package names like ubuntu-* metapackages and apport/motd references.

### Gaps
- EU/UK Russia software sanctions primary text, GPL and codec primary pages, PayPal supported countries, OFAC general licences: not fetched or fetch failed.
- Whether the BIS ENC notification (742.15(b)) is needed for Nubo-specific crypto code: likely none unless Nubo ships non-standard cryptography; not verified.
