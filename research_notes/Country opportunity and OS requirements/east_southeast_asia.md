# East Asia and Southeast Asia: market opportunity and OS-distribution requirements (as of October 2026)

Method note: web-search budget ran out after ~6 searches, so legal columns for several countries rest on my background knowledge and are marked "(background, not verified this session)". Cited items were fetched this session. Population/internet figures are DataReportal Digital 2026 (data as of Oct 2025). Desktop OS shares are StatCounter, September 2026 (monthly). StatCounter is a panel of sites that run its tag, so small-country figures (Brunei, Laos, Timor-Leste, Macau, Mongolia, Myanmar, Cambodia) and "Linux" shares are noisy. "OS X" and "macOS" are listed separately by StatCounter (both are Apple; I sum them as "Apple"). StatCounter explains a known classification quirk: newer macOS reports as "OS X" in many browsers, so the split is not meaningful.

## 1. Which countries are the best targets, and why?

### Takeaway
Best targets: Singapore, Taiwan, Japan, Hong Kong and South Korea for high income and dev density; Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam and Thailand for scale. Thailand has the highest Linux desktop share in the region (10.7%), Indonesia 4.4%, Taiwan 4.2%. China is a distinct, restricted market; Myanmar and North Korea are blocked in practice. Recommended first wave: Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, Indonesia (English-first launch plus CJK/Thai/Bahasa input), then Japan, Malaysia, Philippines, Hong Kong.

### Inferences
- Linux share (StatCounter, noisy) is highest in Thailand 10.7%, Myanmar 7.2% (likely noise), Japan 7.8% (Aug), Malaysia 6.1%, Singapore 5.7%, Hong Kong 5.4%, Macau 4.6%, Indonesia 4.4%, Taiwan 4.2%. Treat as a hint of developer/enthusiast density, not a market size.
- Market-size tiers: scale (Indonesia 230 m online, Philippines 98 m, Vietnam 86 m, Thailand 68 m), high-income dev-heavy (Japan 107 m, Korea 51 m, Taiwan 22 m, Singapore 5.8 m, HK 7.2 m), tiny/frontier (Brunei, Timor-Leste, Laos, Cambodia, Mongolia).
- Windows is 70-92% everywhere, so the realistic path is dual-boot/VM/ChromeOS-style replacement for older PCs; Philippines/Malaysia ChromeOS share (6.8%/5.1%) suggests education-sector hardware that Nubo could target later.
- Singapore, Hong Kong, Macau have the highest Apple share (19-23%), i.e. a premium audience; Apple users are better reached by cloud-account apps (mail/Drive clients) than by the OS itself.

## 2. What local requirements change the product (languages, input, residency, entity/licence)?

### Takeaway
Product changes: full CJK, Thai, Khmer, Lao, Burmese, Vietnamese support with IBus/Fcitx5 input methods preinstalled per locale; data residency matters in China, Vietnam (and Indonesia for public/strategic data, with a regulator still being built); a local entity/representative is needed in Korea (above thresholds), probably Vietnam, and is a hard requirement in China. Most other markets accept offshore hosting with PDPA-style safeguards.

### Cited Findings
- China: personal information export needs one of CAC security assessment, CAC standard contract filing, or personal-information-protection certification (PIPL Art. 38). Certification measures took effect 1 Jan 2026; certification route covers non-sensitive PI of 100k to under 1m persons or sensitive PI under 10k persons per year, for non-CIIO entities with no important data — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/china/privacy/cross-border-transfer-of-personal-information-china's-new-measure-on-certification), [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/china-cross-border-data-transfer-certification/).
- Vietnam: Personal Data Protection Law (Law 91/2025/QH15) effective 1 Jan 2026; Law on Data (60/2024) effective 1 Jul 2025 (important/core data classes); revised Cybersecurity Law approved 10 Dec 2025, effective 1 Jul 2026, requires localised storage of user data for telecom/internet service providers; a draft Law on Data Security (assessment released 14 Jul 2026, expected on the Oct 2026 National Assembly agenda) would ban core-data export — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320523/20260715/vietnam-bans-core-data-exports-fourth-data-law-security-ministry-controls-transfers.htm). Cross-border transfer requires an impact-assessment dossier filed within 60 days of first transfer; fines up to 5% of prior-year revenue or VND 3 bn — [DFDL / CookieYes via search](https://www.dfdl.com/insights/legal-and-tax-updates/vietnam-personal-data-protection-2026-what-foreign-organizations-need-to-know/). TechTimes is a secondary news source; confirm with Vietnamese counsel.
- Indonesia: PDP Law (27/2022) fully applicable since Oct 2024; implementing GR 33/2026 signed 16 Jul 2026, in force ~16 Jan 2027; extraterritorial; no general localisation mandate (regulator may add), no explicit registration, fines up to 2% of annual gross revenue; transfer mechanisms (adequacy, SCCs) not yet operable because the supervisory body does not exist — [Nusantara DFDL](https://www.nusantaradfdl.com/insights/indonesia-personal-data-protection-law-gr-33-2026/), [Adaptist](https://adaptistconsulting.com/reports/indonesias-pdp-law-is-fully-enforceable-without-regulator/). GR 71/2019 localisation applies to public electronic system operators' strategic data — [Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/insights/data-protected/data-protected---indonesia).
- Korea: PIPA amended; overseas controllers without a Korean office must appoint a domestic agent if they hit prescribed thresholds (KRW 1 tn sales or 1 m+ Korean data subjects daily average); deadline to appoint a qualifying entity was 2 Apr 2026; overseas transfer allowed to equivalent-protection countries or certified companies; large turnover-based fines (up to ~10% in 2026 commentary) — [Nomardy](https://kj.nomardy.com/korea-pipa-compliance-for-foreign-companies/), [StartCompanyKorea](https://www.startcompanykorea.com/posts/korea-pipa-2026-10-percent-fines-foreign-startups/) (commentary sites, medium reliability). Cloud security assurance (CSAP) applies to public-sector cloud buyers (background, not verified this session).
- Thailand: PDPA enforced since June 2022; cross-border transfers need adequacy or safeguards (SCCs, ASEAN MCCs, BCRs) or consent; no explicit localisation; no controller registration; fines up to THB 5 m administrative plus criminal exposure; 72 h breach notice; 6 fine cases to date (1 in 2024, 5 in 2025) — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=TH).
- Myanmar: Cybersecurity Law 1/2025 (enacted 1 Jan 2025, commencement by presidential notice) requires digital platforms with 100k+ users to register as a Myanmar company and with the department, data retention up to three years, criminalises unauthorised VPN service — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f9cee2e1-6fff-4f55-a958-a73f4a6a6014), [IFEX](https://ifex.org/myanmar-junta-enacts-cybersecurity-law-imposing-penalties-for-unauthorised-vpn-use/).
- Languages/scripts (background, not verified this session, standard facts): Simplified Chinese (China, Singapore), Traditional Chinese (Taiwan, HK, Macau; Cantonese in HK/Macau), Japanese, Korean Hangul, Mongolian Cyrillic, Bahasa Indonesia, Bahasa Melayu (Latin; Jawi optional in Brunei), Thai, Vietnamese (Latin with stacked diacritics), Filipino/Tagalog and English, Khmer, Lao, Burmese (Unicode vs legacy Zawgyi font issue), Tetum and Portuguese (Timor-Leste), Korean regular IME needs.

### Inferences
- Ubuntu/GNOME ships IBus; install ibus-libpinyin/Rime, ibus-mozc, ibus-hangul, ibus-chewing/ibus-table-cangjie (HK/Macau/Taiwan), ibus-unikey/ibus-bamboo (Vietnamese), ibus-typing-booster or m17n (Thai/Khmer/Lao/Burmese). Wayland + GNOME 50 IBus compatibility should be tested for CJK candidate windows, plus Noto CJK/Thai/Khmer/Lao/Myanmar fonts in the image. In China, Fcitx5 users are common (background); ship both as options.
- Hosting at mail.nubo.email from India/elsewhere is workable with SCC/consent mechanisms in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Philippines, Japan, Korea (with representative above thresholds). A launch-small posture is below Korea's thresholds. Vietnam and China need legal advice or are better excluded initially.
- India-based operator sending data to Indonesia/Vietnam residents' mailboxes: monitor Indonesia GR 33 transfer rules after Jan 2027.

## 3. Where is distribution or payment blocked or high risk?

### Takeaway
China is not blocked for an open-source OS download, but offering a cloud account (mail/files) to Chinese residents requires local licensing, ICP filing, data localisation and a local partner, making it effectively blocked for Nubo; the government is also accelerating domestic Linux (Kylin/UOS). North Korea is blocked by sanctions. Myanmar is high risk (military regime law, sanctions, payments).

### Cited Findings
- China orders: Ministry of State Security reportedly told some state-affiliated entities to remove Windows 10 China Government Edition and move to domestic Linux (Kylin OS by CEC, UOS by UnionTech); original end date Feb 2027 pulled forward to 2026; applies to government/state entities only, consumer Windows Home China still available; hurdles: application testing, driver validation, retraining — [Soyacincau, 24 Aug 2026](https://soyacincau.com/2026/08/24/china-orders-state-agencies-to-ditch-windows/), [Wikipedia: Linux adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption), [Karmactive](https://www.karmactive.com/china-windows-exit-kylin-os-uos-xinchuang-technology-independence/). Based on news reports; official text not seen.
- Myanmar: see section 2 (platform registration, VPN criminalisation).
- South Korea: May 2019 announcement of migrating major government systems to Linux — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_adoption) (2019, outcome not verified).

### Inferences / background (not verified this session)
- China: Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, PIPL; cloud/email counts as value-added telecom, needing MIIT licence (foreign equity restricted, historically via a local partner) plus ICP filing; MLPS 2.0 graded protection for network operators; Commercial Cryptography Law governs crypto products. Practical result: Blocked/not recommended; a downloadable ISO from outside China is not itself licensed but accounts/mail hosted abroad for Chinese users are legally grey and technically unreliable (GFW). Payments: foreign cards rarely work for Chinese residents; Alipay/WeChat Pay require a local entity.
- North Korea: UN/US/EU sanctions and US export controls; blocked, no payment rails. Do not distribute or market there. Red Star OS is the state OS (background).
- Myanmar: US/EU/UK sanctions on military-linked entities and banks; limited card acceptance and forex controls; registration requirement above 100k users; surveillance risk to users' mailboxes. Rate Low/High-risk.
- Hardware bundles (later): type approval relevant for radio equipment (e.g., Korea KC/ KCC, Japan MIC/TELEC, China CCC/SRRC, Indonesia SDPPI/TKDN local-content rules, Vietnam MIC), not verified.
- Payments (background): Singapore/HK/Japan/Korea/Taiwan/Malaysia/Thailand cards work; Indonesia and Vietnam have heavy wallet/bank-transfer/QR use (GoPay/OVO/DANA, MoMo/ZaloPay), Philippines GCash/Maya, Thailand PromptPay, Malaysia DuitNow; Stripe/Razorpay-style gateways support cards in SG, HK, JP, MY, TH (Stripe), ID and PH only via local acquirers/entities (not verified). Indonesia and Vietnam impose VAT/digital-services tax on foreign digital providers (not verified). FX limits: Vietnam, Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia dollarised/controlled, Mongolia tugrik controlled (not verified).

### Gaps
- No search on OFAC/EU Myanmar and North Korea sanctions details; no source on China MIIT/VATS licence or MLPS this session; no payment source for any country. These are listed as background, not verified.

## Most promising countries (narrative)
1. Singapore: highest internet penetration (98.4%), 5.7% Linux, 23% Apple, English-speaking, strong card payments, PDPA permits offshore hosting with accountability. Small but good beachhead and credibility hub for ASEAN.
2. Thailand: highest Linux desktop share (10.7%) and 68 m online; needs good Thai input and fonts; PDPA workable.
3. Taiwan: 22 m online, 4.2% Linux, tech-savvy; Traditional Chinese quality is the product bar.
4. Indonesia: largest SEA market (230 m online, 4.4% Linux); price sensitivity (wallets, low ARPU) and incoming GR 33 (Jan 2027) are the watch items.
5. Japan and Korea: big, wealthy, but entrenched local ecosystems; Japan Linux 7.8% (Aug, noisy); consider after Taiwan/Singapore proof points.
6. Malaysia, Philippines, Hong Kong: solid second wave.

## Most restricted
- China: PIPL/DSL/CSL with export mechanisms, licensing, domestic-OS push (cited above). Do not target.
- North Korea: sanctions (background). Block downloads and sign-ups.
- Myanmar: military cybersecurity law with platform registration and VPN offences (cited), sanctions and payments constraints (background).
- Vietnam: tightening, localisation and a pending fourth data law in the Oct 2026 session (cited); medium until a lawyer clears it.
